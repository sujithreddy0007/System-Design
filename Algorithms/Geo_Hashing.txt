Geohashing
Definition

Geohashing is a technique that converts a latitude and longitude into a short string representing a geographic area.

How It Works

The Earth's surface is recursively divided into smaller grid cells. Each cell gets a unique hash string.

World
  ↓
Large Grid
  ↓
Smaller Grid
  ↓
Smaller Grid
  ↓
Geohash

Example:

Latitude: 17.385
Longitude: 78.486


        ↓


Geohash: "te..."
Key Property

Nearby locations generally have similar geohash prefixes.

For example:

te7abc
te7abd
te7abe

These locations are likely geographically close.

Why Use It?

Instead of calculating distance against every location:

All Drivers
    ↓
Geohash nearby cells
    ↓
Candidate Drivers
    ↓
Exact distance calculation

This greatly reduces the search space.

Use Cases
Uber / Lyft → Nearby drivers
Food delivery → Nearby restaurants
Location-based search
Maps
Geospatial databases
Limitation

Geohash prefixes do not always perfectly represent physical proximity, especially around cell boundaries.

Therefore, systems usually search the target cell and neighboring cells.

Interview One-Liner

Geohashing converts geographic coordinates into hierarchical grid identifiers, allowing efficient proximity searches by comparing geohash prefixes.
